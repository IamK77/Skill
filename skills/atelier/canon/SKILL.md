# canon

Generate visual points of view and unexpected design languages from a product’s tensions and meaning. Use for creative direction, not an upstream certificate required by other lenses.

## Starting point

A point of view is a way of making some aspect of the subject newly visible. It may emerge from a product fact, a cultural association, or a deliberate tension; it is not a slogan to manufacture on demand.

Bring a question, fragment, reference, or tension. Follow whichever association becomes useful; these invitations are not a sequence to complete. Speculation should remain distinguishable from an observed fact.

## Thought experiments

### Change what is foreground

Imagine the product, the person using it, and the consequence of use each taking the leading role. Sketch a different visual premise for each.

### Borrow an organising culture

Try a field guide, a score, a workshop, an archive, or something less obvious. Transfer how it organises attention, not just its typography.

### Contradict the obvious style

Name the expected visual treatment and make one plausible opposite. Keep it long enough to discover something, rather than rejecting it for unfamiliarity.

## A possible turn

An outreach tool could celebrate effortless generation, or make careful reading visible as margin notes. That shift changes the visual subject from output volume to attention. A third direction might treat correspondence as a personal archive rather than a campaign.

## Leave the space open

Offer a few genuinely different possibilities, a sketch, an analogy, or a sharper question when useful. Do not turn every prompt into a scored diagnostic, a completion gate, or a demand for an action plan. The human may choose a direction, combine them, or leave the question open.

Other atelier lenses can develop an interesting thread without running all lenses. The optional probe detects declared surface patterns; it does not judge taste or originality.
