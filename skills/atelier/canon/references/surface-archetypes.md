# Archetypes as borrowing grounds

## Try a family resemblance

A dashboard foregrounds current signals; a tool foregrounds manipulation; editorial work foregrounds a reading sequence; marketing work foregrounds an invitation; a data-dense workspace foregrounds comparison. These are starting metaphors, not mutually exclusive bins or templates the product must fit.

## Change the dominant relationship

A dashboard can borrow annotations from editorial work so anomalies acquire context. A document can borrow filters from a tool so readers pursue their own questions. A marketing page can borrow an instrument's honest demonstration rather than promise an outcome in abstract copy.

## Example

A project-status screen made of identical cards suggests an inventory. A timeline suggests sequence and dependency. A shared notebook suggests discussion and uncertainty. Each can contain the same underlying information while proposing a different activity.

## Sketch a contrast

Use real content and sketch two arrangements that differ in what the reader does first. Explore how each treats an empty result, a long label, or an unfinished task. These cases can generate a composition rather than merely test the limits of a hero screen.

## Keep the borrowing selective

Do not inherit every convention of the source genre. Dense is not automatically serious; sparse is not automatically premium. Once a direction is wanted, test its actual reading and interaction requirements. There is no archetype certificate to obtain before using another visual lens.
