# Edges, shadows, and separation

## An opening

Imagine a panel as an inset tray, a sheet resting above a table, and a purely typographic section. Each gives a different reason for an edge or shadow. A consistent imagined light source can help an object-like direction, while a deliberately flat direction may need none of it.

## Elaborate a candidate

A chosen object-like direction can use a consistent imagined light source to relate shadows and highlights. Distinguish the purpose of elevation from the effect used to depict it: spacing, borders, surface values, or overlap may establish the same separation without a shadow. View the result against its actual background and theme.

## A small comparison

Place an inset region, a floating panel, and a modal in one specimen. Make their different relationships legible rather than merely assigning three larger blur radii. A flat alternative can be equally coherent if it preserves the required distinctions.

Use this note when its question is useful, not as a required inspection. A sketch, a contrast, or an unresolved question can be the result. The [parent lens](../SKILL.md) offers other ways into the subject; execution begins only when a direction and task are chosen.
