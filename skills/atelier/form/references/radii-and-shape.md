# A family of shapes and intentional exceptions

## An opening

Draw the same control as a cut-out, a folded tab, and a smooth pebble. Explore which relationships among containers and controls make each family coherent. Nested-radius formulas can help elaborate one family; optical exceptions or asymmetry may express the idea better than numerical uniformity.

## Elaborate a candidate

Relate corners, edges, outlines, and nested containers to the material or character being explored. A geometric nested-radius calculation can be a starting point when two rounded boundaries should appear concentric, but stroke width, padding, and optical judgement affect the rendered result. Not every component needs the same silhouette.

## A small comparison

Compare one nested card/control pair at several sizes with actual content. Try sharp containment around a soft control, then a consistently rounded family. Preserve the contrast if it expresses a useful hierarchy rather than averaging every shape into numerical uniformity.

## Keep the choice open

Use this note when its question is useful, not as a required inspection. A sketch, a contrast, or an unresolved question can be the result. The [parent lens](../SKILL.md) offers other ways into the subject; execution begins only when a direction and task are chosen.
