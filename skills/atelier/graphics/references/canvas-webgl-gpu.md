# Develop a visual process before a renderer

## An opening

Start from a visual process: growth, interference, weaving, erosion, or accumulating traces. Express it as a small changing field or repeated mark before deciding whether a shader is useful. A simple two-dimensional construction may be more expressive than a technically elaborate scene.

## Elaborate a candidate

Express the candidate as marks, transformations, and a time or interaction model. A small two-dimensional prototype can clarify the process before GPU machinery is useful. If a GPU renderer is chosen, investigate data transfer, shader work, resolution, device limits, and fallback behaviour in the actual environment instead of assuming it is automatically faster.

## A small comparison

A field of growing traces might work as a static illustration, a lightweight canvas drawing, or a shader-based effect. Compare what each contributes to the idea and what it costs to interact with or explain. Keep the essential meaning available outside the most elaborate rendering path.

## Keep the choice open

Use this note when its question is useful, not as a required inspection. A sketch, a contrast, or an unresolved question can be the result. The [parent lens](../SKILL.md) offers other ways into the subject; execution begins only when a direction and task are chosen.
