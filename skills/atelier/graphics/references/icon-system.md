# Marks, labels, and a coherent family

## An opening

Invent a small grammar of marks rather than collecting unrelated symbols. Try changing how direction, containment, interruption, and completion are expressed while keeping the family recognisable. A successful metaphor can still need a label when people interpret it differently.

## Elaborate a candidate

Choose a construction grammar such as stroke, fill, corner treatment, viewpoint, and optical size. Apply it to a small representative set that includes difficult meanings, not only obvious arrows. Preserve labels or other context when interpretation is ambiguous; consistency of drawing does not guarantee consistency of understanding.

## A small comparison

Compare icons for pause, stop, cancel, and dismiss in the actual control context. A beautiful family can still blur those different actions. Revise the metaphor, add language, or use another affordance rather than assuming more decorative detail will make the meaning universal.

## Keep the choice open

Use this note when its question is useful, not as a required inspection. A sketch, a contrast, or an unresolved question can be the result. The [parent lens](../SKILL.md) offers other ways into the subject; execution begins only when a direction and task are chosen.
