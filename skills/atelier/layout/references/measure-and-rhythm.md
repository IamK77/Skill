# Cadence through space and density

## An opening

Read the content as a sequence of breaths, interruptions, and returns. Try a dense passage next to a pause, then a more even cadence. The question is how the rhythm changes attention; a spacing scale is an instrument for reproducing a chosen cadence, not its source.

## Elaborate a candidate

Relate measure, leading, group spacing, and section transitions to the content and intended pace. A small spacing vocabulary can support coherence, but meaningful grouping and optical relationships sometimes need exceptions. Density is neither intrinsically professional nor intrinsically hostile; test the intended reading and interaction.

## A small comparison

Set a real paragraph, table, and interruption in two different rhythms. Compare a steady cadence with a deliberately spacious pause around an important observation. Record a chosen relationship as a specimen rather than treating a token scale as proof of the composition.

## Keep the choice open

Use this note when its question is useful, not as a required inspection. A sketch, a contrast, or an unresolved question can be the result. The [parent lens](../SKILL.md) offers other ways into the subject; execution begins only when a direction and task are chosen.
