# color

Explore palette ideas through perception, material associations, temperature, and semantic relationships. Use for generative colour thinking; contrast measurements are optional instruments, not taste gates.

## Starting point

Colour is relational: a quiet hue can become loud in the right company. Explore what the palette makes possible before turning it into a fixed ramp.

Bring a question, fragment, reference, or tension. Follow whichever association becomes useful; these invitations are not a sequence to complete. Speculation should remain distinguishable from an observed fact.

## Thought experiments

### Move the light source

Imagine the same subject under morning light, a monitor glow, and reflected water. Translate those relationships into background, foreground, and accent possibilities.

### Give colour a grammar

Try colour as material identity, emotional punctuation, or data meaning. Explore what should remain neutral under each grammar.

### Change the neighbour

Keep one colour fixed and change its surroundings. Discover a direction through interaction rather than collecting attractive swatches independently.

## A possible turn

A dark interface need not be a daytime palette inverted. A night-observatory metaphor suggests restrained luminous marks on cool darkness; a theatre metaphor suggests warm pools of attention. Both can be explored before choosing and measuring actual text/background pairs.

## Leave the space open

Offer a few genuinely different possibilities, a sketch, an analogy, or a sharper question when useful. Do not turn every prompt into a scored diagnostic, a completion gate, or a demand for an action plan. The human may choose a direction, combine them, or leave the question open.

Use the coherence probe on a rendered candidate if useful. It measures declared relationships, not aesthetic merit; implementation still needs context-appropriate accessibility checks.
