# Give ambiguous gestures an explicit meaning

## An opening

Imagine one gesture as direct manipulation, then as a command, then as navigation through a larger space. Those meanings create different expectations about ownership and interruption. Sketch the ambiguous moment where dragging, scrolling, or selection could all be plausible before choosing an interaction contract.

## Elaborate a candidate

Identify which interaction owns a gesture in the actual context and what happens when a person changes direction, interrupts, or leaves the target. Compare direct manipulation with command or navigation interpretations. Preserve other required input modes and avoid assuming that one pointer gesture is universally discoverable.

## A small comparison

On a horizontally draggable object inside a vertically scrolling view, sketch the ambiguous initial movement. Compare delayed commitment, an explicit handle, and a different interaction. Once selected, test the contract on representative devices instead of relying on a purely visual animation prototype.

## Keep the choice open

Use this note when its question is useful, not as a required inspection. A sketch, a contrast, or an unresolved question can be the result. The [parent lens](../SKILL.md) offers other ways into the subject; execution begins only when a direction and task are chosen.
