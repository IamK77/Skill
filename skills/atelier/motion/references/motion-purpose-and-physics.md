# Timing models as expressive tools

## An opening

Give a transition the weight of paper, a precise mechanism, or a buoyant object, then remove the literal physics and keep only the useful rhythm. Ask what continuity or character remains. A spring is one expressive model, not proof that movement will feel natural.

## Elaborate a candidate

A curve, spring, or state transition can express continuity and character, but the parameters are not a universal account of physical or perceptual correctness. Consider interruption and retargeting, not just a transition played once from the beginning. The motion should remain understandable when the action repeats or another event arrives.

## A small comparison

Compare a deliberate settling movement with a brisk acknowledgment for the same task. Then remove the movement and preserve the information. This contrast can reveal what the animation contributes before an implementation budget or library choice becomes relevant.

## Keep the choice open

Use this note when its question is useful, not as a required inspection. A sketch, a contrast, or an unresolved question can be the result. The [parent lens](../SKILL.md) offers other ways into the subject; execution begins only when a direction and task are chosen.
