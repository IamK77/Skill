# Features that support actual reading

## An opening

Keep the font family fixed and explore numerals, alternates, weight, spacing, and case. A small feature change can suggest a different narrator without a new typeface. Compare actual words and data, not isolated glyphs; optical judgement is contextual rather than a list of premium details.

## Elaborate a candidate

Compare the font's available numeral styles, alternates, weight, spacing, and punctuation with real content. Tabular numerals may support aligned changing values; distinguishable zero and letter forms may help identifiers. Those are use-specific choices, not mandatory premium details or a universal list of good typography.

## A small comparison

Render a table of mixed-length numbers, an identifier, and a paragraph with the candidate settings. Inspect the result at the intended size and under fallback. Keep a feature because it supports the task or voice, not merely because the font exposes it.

## Keep the choice open

Use this note when its question is useful, not as a required inspection. A sketch, a contrast, or an unresolved question can be the result. The [parent lens](../SKILL.md) offers other ways into the subject; execution begins only when a direction and task are chosen.
