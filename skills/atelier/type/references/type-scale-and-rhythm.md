# Hierarchy and cadence before a ratio

## An opening

Cast the same content as a ledger, a letter, and a field guide. Change hierarchy, measure, rhythm, or weight to express the distinction. The useful idea may be a different relationship with the reader rather than a more dramatic modular ratio.

## Elaborate a candidate

Relate sizes, weights, line height, and measure to the voice and reading task. A modular ratio can generate a starting set, but role differences and optical adjustments may justify exceptions. Start with a purposeful set of families; family count alone does not establish coherence and there is no universal ceiling.

## A small comparison

Use actual headings, a paragraph, a long label, and data to compare two hierarchies. Check resizing, wrapping, fallback, and the product's readability requirements when implementing the chosen direction. A fluid-size formula should be tested in the relevant zoom and viewport conditions rather than treated as automatically accessible.

## Keep the choice open

Use this note when its question is useful, not as a required inspection. A sketch, a contrast, or an unresolved question can be the result. The [parent lens](../SKILL.md) offers other ways into the subject; execution begins only when a direction and task are chosen.
