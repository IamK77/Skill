# Places where new questions can emerge

## Follow a seam that is interesting

Possibilities often appear between objective and measurement, theory and implementation, a benchmark and its intended population, one field's vocabulary and another's, short-run behaviour and long-run dynamics, a successful average and a failing subgroup, or a proposed capability and the resources needed to use it.

These are invitations, not seven boxes to inspect for every project. Start with the seam that makes an actual observation puzzling.

## Translate twice

Take one relation from another domain. First make a literal translation and notice where it breaks. Then transfer only the underlying relation—feedback, memory, competition, conservation, or coordination. The second translation may produce a more useful mechanism than the themed analogy.

## Example

A benchmark rewards rapid adaptation after a distribution shift. An ecological-succession analogy suggests multiple time scales and a changing population of strategies. A control-theory analogy suggests feedback delay and stability. The two frames produce different questions about the same result.

## Preserve alternatives

Record why a question is interesting, what source or observation inspired it, and which assumption it would change. Keep empirical claims separate from imaginative transfers. Choosing a feasible experiment can happen later, once the human wants to pursue a direction.
