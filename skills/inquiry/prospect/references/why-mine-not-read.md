# Reading for friction and possibility

## Two useful modes

Reading to understand an established result and reading to generate a new question are different activities. Neither is universally better. When exploring, follow places where two descriptions fail to fit together: a simplifying assumption, an omitted actor, an unexplained failure, or a method borrowed across an awkward boundary.

## Change the unit of attention

Instead of collecting papers by topic, follow a mechanism, an unusual measurement, a dataset convention, or a recurring exception. A bibliography can then become a map of competing explanations rather than a list of summaries.

## Example

A scheduling literature treats setup cost as a constant. Looking through a learning or adaptation lens suggests that setup history may change the cost itself. A market lens instead suggests a price for switching. These associations open different questions before either becomes a research proposal.

## Keep a trace without forcing a verdict

A short note can preserve the source, the observation, the association it triggered, and what remains speculative. Do not turn every reading into a scored novelty claim. If a direction attracts sustained interest, prospect's other notes can help develop it; an unresolved tension is also a useful result.
