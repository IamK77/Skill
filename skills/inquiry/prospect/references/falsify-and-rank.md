# Contrast questions without prematurely closing them

## Imagine an inconvenient observation

For a promising explanation, invent a case in which it should stop working. That case can sharpen the mechanism or become a new research subject. Falsification here is a way to elaborate a question, not a mandatory obstacle course for every early idea.

## Compare along different axes

A question may be surprising, useful, tractable, conceptually clarifying, or well matched to available data. These values can conflict. Instead of collapsing them into an unexplained score, describe what each candidate offers and what pursuing it would leave aside.

## Example

One candidate could improve a benchmark quickly; another could explain why several methods fail under delayed feedback. A third may require unavailable longitudinal data but reveal a more consequential phenomenon. A small feasibility study might clarify one uncertainty without deciding which question is intrinsically most valuable.

## When a choice is requested

Name the decision context and resource constraints, then offer a recommendation with the reasons and uncertainties intact. If no choice is requested, return contrasting questions or a landscape of possibilities. Novelty and value are not certificates that this note can issue.
