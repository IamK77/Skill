# Removing pieces to discover what an idea means

## Use subtraction generatively

Remove a component, replace it with a simpler one, or give another component its role. Before treating this as a formal evaluation, ask whether the altered mechanism suggests a better or more interesting explanation.

## Example

A hybrid method combines a learned proposal with a memory of failures. Removing memory might reveal that diversity comes from the proposal itself. Replacing the learned proposal with a simple sampler might reveal that memory is the essential mechanism. Either result can generate a different research direction rather than merely defend the original design.

## Distinguish resemblance from contribution

Compare relations and assumptions, not only names and implementation details. An idea can resemble earlier work yet contribute a new regime, explanation, measurement, or constraint. Conversely, new terminology does not establish novelty.

## When the human chooses to test a claim

A formal ablation needs a comparison design that controls meaningful differences, records compute and data boundaries, and allows an unfavourable result. Ledger and forge support that execution. During ideation, a thought experiment or a small exploratory prototype may be enough to expose a new possibility.

Keep references attributable and uncertainty visible. This note does not certify novelty or require every speculative idea to survive a tournament before it can be discussed.
