# A mechanism sketch and its possible limits

## Sketch enough to make the idea distinct

Describe the objects, the transformation, and the information available at each step. Include one small worked situation that distinguishes the idea from an existing mechanism. This is a thinking artifact, not a compulsory implementation specification.

## Change the limiting resource

Imagine the mechanism under a severe memory limit, delayed information, scarce labels, or an expensive action. Different constraints can suggest different versions of the idea. The interesting limit may concern what can be known or coordinated, not merely runtime complexity.

## Example

A method that recomputes every candidate after new evidence may be elegant but costly. A selective-update version, a compressed memory, and a negotiated approximation each change what the method means. Keep those alternatives visible rather than quietly presenting one as the original idea.

## Separate kinds of ceiling

A proven bound depends on explicit assumptions. An estimated cost depends on the proposed implementation and environment. A failed small experiment is an observation, not necessarily an impossibility result. Record the distinction and use limits to generate the next question.

A selected mechanism can become a versioned plan in ledger; a sketch with unresolved boundaries is also a legitimate outcome.
