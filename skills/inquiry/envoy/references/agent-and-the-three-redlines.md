# Claim, citation, and authorship integrity

## Keep three sources of authority separate

Experimental artifacts support observations. Cited sources support statements about other work. Authors decide what they are willing to claim and submit. None substitutes for another.

## Work from the evidence

For a quantitative statement, preserve the route from data and exclusions through analysis to the displayed number. For a literature statement, inspect the actual source and verify that it supports the wording. A plausible title or familiar author is not a citation.

## Handle gaps explicitly

If a result was not run, describe it as proposed or unavailable. If a citation cannot be verified, remove or qualify the unsupported claim rather than invent bibliographic details. If authors disagree about interpretation, record the issue for decision instead of manufacturing consensus.

## Example

A draft says a method improves every instance, but the aggregate table only reports a mean. Inspect the per-instance results. The justified claim may be an average improvement with several regressions, which is both more precise and more useful to a reader.

## Completion boundary

A prepared manuscript or response is not an authorised submission. Keep changes attributable and ask the responsible authors before external communication. These are concrete provenance and authority requirements, not assertions about any model's character.
