# Different meanings of better

## An opening

Imagine “better” as lower effort, clearer understanding, greater control, or faster coordination. These objectives can disagree. A different objective may produce a different experience rather than an optimization of the current screen. Keep the product owner’s choice distinct from a design hypothesis.

## Elaborate a candidate

Clarify whether the proposed experience aims to reduce effort, support learning, increase control, improve coordination, or another chosen outcome. Those values can conflict, and a metric is only one representation of them. Keep product decisions attributable rather than treating a technical objective function as the user's intent.

## A small comparison

An onboarding flow can optimize rapid completion or careful understanding of consequential choices. Compare those experiences with the same real task. The useful result may be a question about what the product should prioritize rather than a numeric ranking of screens.

## Keep the choice open

Use this note when its question is useful, not as a required inspection. A sketch, a contrast, or an unresolved question can be the result. The [parent lens](../SKILL.md) offers other ways into the subject; execution begins only when a direction and task are chosen.
