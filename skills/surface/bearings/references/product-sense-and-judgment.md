# Use context without inventing the requirement

## An opening

Imagine the product from the perspective of a first-time visitor, a returning expert, and someone interrupted halfway through. Invent a different organising activity for each rather than averaging their needs into one dashboard. The contrast may expose a product decision worth asking about.

## Elaborate a candidate

Relate a design proposal to a specific activity, audience, and consequence. Use existing product evidence where available and distinguish it from a creative hypothesis. A familiar convention may aid recognition, while a different structure may reveal a more useful activity; investigate rather than treating either as inherently right.

## A small comparison

A novice may need an explanation of a result while an expert needs comparison across many results. Instead of averaging them into one compromise dashboard, explore a progressive relationship or distinct views. Bring the consequential choice back to the product owner.

## Keep the choice open

Use this note when its question is useful, not as a required inspection. A sketch, a contrast, or an unresolved question can be the result. The [parent lens](../SKILL.md) offers other ways into the subject; execution begins only when a direction and task are chosen.
