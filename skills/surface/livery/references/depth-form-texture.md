# Material as a compositional choice

## An opening

Imagine the interface as layers of paper, cut apertures, or luminous marks on a flat plane. Compare how each expresses containment and importance. Texture or depth can be absent by design; optical consistency does not require a universal physical simulation.

## Elaborate a candidate

Relate surfaces, edges, shadows, light, and texture to the chosen premise. A flat plane, layered paper, and a solid object can each express containment differently. Keep informational distinctions and readable content intact, and test optical details in the rendered context rather than declaring one radius or shadow recipe universal.

## A small comparison

Compare a nested panel as an inset tray, a sheet resting above another, and a typographic group with no object boundary. The useful choice may remove depth entirely. Atelier form can develop the relationship without requiring every visual dimension to be revised.

## Keep the choice open

Use this note when its question is useful, not as a required inspection. A sketch, a contrast, or an unresolved question can be the result. The [parent lens](../SKILL.md) offers other ways into the subject; execution begins only when a direction and task are chosen.
