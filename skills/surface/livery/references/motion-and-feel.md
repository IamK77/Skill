# Character through timing and continuity

## An opening

Give the interface a patient, brisk, or playful rhythm while keeping the task unchanged. Explore how anticipation, interruption, and a quiet ending alter that character. Then imagine the still equivalent; it can reveal which meaning truly depends on motion.

## Elaborate a candidate

Explore anticipation, movement, interruption, and ending as separate expressive choices. Keep the underlying operation's outcome distinguishable from its animation. A selected motion needs relevant preference handling, input alternatives, and measured performance, but the creative exploration can remain a timing sketch.

## A small comparison

Try a task completion as a brisk acknowledgment, a deliberate settling into history, and a quiet still change. Compare how each affects repeated work. The most expressive version may be the one that avoids animation while preserving continuity.

## Keep the choice open

Use this note when its question is useful, not as a required inspection. A sketch, a contrast, or an unresolved question can be the result. The [parent lens](../SKILL.md) offers other ways into the subject; execution begins only when a direction and task are chosen.
