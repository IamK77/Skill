# Colour and light as a shared visual premise

## Borrow a kind of illumination

Imagine the subject in a library reading room, an observatory, or a workshop window. Translate relationships such as warm pools of attention, distant luminous marks, or directional edge contrast. The result need not simulate the place literally.

## Keep two alternatives alive

Try colour as the identity of a material, then as punctuation within a mostly neutral surface. In the first, large areas can carry character; in the second, a small accent can carry emphasis. There is no universal area percentage that decides which is correct.

## Example

A document-review tool can resemble paper under warm reflected light or a precise illuminated instrument. Apply each idea to the same actual paragraph, comment, and selected passage. This reveals whether colour and light change the reading experience, not merely whether a swatch looks attractive.

## Technique for a selected candidate

A perceptual colour space can make relationships easier to manipulate, but a coordinate is not an accessibility verdict. Define semantic roles, inspect the rendered combinations, and measure the text, controls, and non-text distinctions required by the product. Treat light and dark themes as separately tested mappings of those roles.

Shadows, highlights, surface values, and borders can express depth together or independently. A flat design may not need a physical light model. Keep neutral or tinted endpoints as options rather than declaring pure black, white, or grey defective.
