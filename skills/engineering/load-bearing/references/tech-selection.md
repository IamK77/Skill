# Compare commitments and reversible experiments

## An opening

Compare technologies as different commitments: what becomes easy to express, what must be operated, and what becomes expensive to leave. Imagine the same product with a familiar tool and an unusual representation. A small seam experiment can clarify a risk without pretending to rank every tool universally.

## Elaborate a candidate

Inspect how a candidate fits the selected model, integration seams, operating environment, team knowledge, and exit cost. Use primary project evidence and a representative trial for current capability claims rather than relying on a technology category or popularity. Keep unknowns explicit.

## A small comparison

Implement one awkward seam with the familiar option and one alternative, then compare the actual compromises. A small spike can settle a compatibility question without pretending to predict every long-term cost. A recommendation should follow the use case and retain its assumptions.

Use this note when its question is useful, not as a required inspection. A sketch, a contrast, or an unresolved question can be the result. The [parent lens](../SKILL.md) offers other ways into the subject; execution begins only when a direction and task are chosen.
