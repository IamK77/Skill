# Record a decision without erasing its alternatives

## An opening

Write two short future histories of a decision: one in which change is frequent and one in which stability matters most. What would each future wish had remained reversible? An architectural note can preserve these alternatives instead of retroactively presenting the chosen design as inevitable.

## Elaborate a candidate

An architectural note can state the context, constraints, selected option, meaningful alternatives, and consequences. Record what evidence informed the choice and what future observation should cause reconsideration. A decision record preserves attribution; it does not prove that the choice was optimal or make later revision a failure.

## A small comparison

Compare a decision optimized for a short-lived experiment with one for a long-lived shared capability. If the experiment succeeds, identify which seam would make migration feasible. Keep the historical rationale when changing the design rather than rewriting the old note as if the new choice had always been obvious.

## Keep the choice open

Use this note when its question is useful, not as a required inspection. A sketch, a contrast, or an unresolved question can be the result. The [parent lens](../SKILL.md) offers other ways into the subject; execution begins only when a direction and task are chosen.
