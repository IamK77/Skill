# Represent questions and histories deliberately

## An opening

Describe the domain once as current records, once as an event history, and once as negotiated claims. Each representation makes different questions natural and preserves a different kind of memory. Let those questions influence the data boundary before choosing a database category.

## Elaborate a candidate

Identify the entities, relationships, identities, invariants, and queries the chosen model needs to express. Compare normalized records, derived projections, histories, and documents as different representations rather than moral categories. Duplication may be an intentional projection with an update contract or an unexplained synchronisation burden.

## A small comparison

A current balance, its transaction history, and a reporting projection answer different questions. Sketch who writes each and what disagreement means. The representation may reveal a useful domain boundary before a database technology is selected.

## Keep the choice open

Use this note when its question is useful, not as a required inspection. A sketch, a contrast, or an unresolved question can be the result. The [parent lens](../SKILL.md) offers other ways into the subject; execution begins only when a direction and task are chosen.
