# Translate a quality into an observable promise

## An opening

Turn a nonfunctional adjective into several possible meanings. “Fast” might mean quick first feedback, low tail latency, or fast recovery after interruption. Each interpretation suggests a different design. Explore the experience that matters before turning a number into an architecture-wide target.

## Elaborate a candidate

Clarify which users, operation, workload, and conditions give a nonfunctional requirement meaning. Latency, throughput, availability, recovery, privacy, and maintainability can conflict and cannot be reduced to one universal architecture score. Do not invent a missing threshold as an industry-normal requirement.

## A small comparison

A long-running report can offer early progress, a revisitable job, or a shorter total computation. Those are different interpretations of fast. Once the human selects the promise, a representative measurement or failure exercise can evaluate a candidate design.

## Keep the choice open

Use this note when its question is useful, not as a required inspection. A sketch, a contrast, or an unresolved question can be the result. The [parent lens](../SKILL.md) offers other ways into the subject; execution begins only when a direction and task are chosen.
