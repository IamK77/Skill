# Questions that expose architectural forks

## An opening

Use the branches as possible questions, not a mandatory route to a single answer. Change the scale, the number of owners, or the cost of a wrong promise, then see which branch becomes interesting. An unresolved fork can identify the small experiment or human decision that would matter most.

## Elaborate a candidate

Ask about a relevant change, failure, ownership split, or resource constraint. Use the answer to open a small set of options rather than classify the entire system. Distinguish a stated requirement from a plausible assumption and bring consequential ambiguity to its owner.

## A small comparison

If two teams need independent release schedules, compare a contract boundary inside one deployment with a separately delivered component. A seam experiment can reveal packaging or compatibility costs. The output can be a sharper question, not necessarily an architectural verdict.

## Keep the choice open

Use this note when its question is useful, not as a required inspection. A sketch, a contrast, or an unresolved question can be the result. The [parent lens](../SKILL.md) offers other ways into the subject; execution begins only when a direction and task are chosen.
