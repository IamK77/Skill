# Architecture as a distribution of future choices

## Change the institution

Imagine the system as a workshop, a postal service, and a marketplace. A workshop suggests shared context and coordinated changes; a postal service suggests explicit addresses and independently delivered messages; a marketplace suggests negotiated contracts. Transfer a relationship, not the whole metaphor.

## Change the future

Describe the same design under three possible changes: the data grows, a second team owns a subsystem, or a policy changes every week. The disagreements reveal different seams. You need not rank the scenarios before noticing what each makes possible.

## Example

A common customer object may seem convenient today. A passport metaphor suggests narrow attestations consumed by separate domains. A case-file metaphor suggests one shared history with custody rules. A local-profile metaphor suggests duplication with explicit reconciliation. These are different meanings of shared identity, not merely three deployment layouts.

## Use technique when a thread earns it

Boundary diagrams, latency experiments, schema sketches, and architectural decision records can sharpen a promising direction. They need not be produced for every possibility. Technical constraints matter when they apply, but neither monoliths nor services are universally virtuous.

## Leave room for the human

Offer distinct alternatives and the assumptions that make each interesting. A selected direction can be handed to groundwork for acceptance examples; an unanswered question is also a useful result.
